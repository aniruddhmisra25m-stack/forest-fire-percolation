Two ways to catch fire

Rule A gives a tree one chance per time step, however many neighbours are burning. Rule B gives it one chance per burning neighbour, so a tree surrounded by fire is more likely to go up. At p = 1 the rules are identical; below that, rule A needs a denser forest before fire can cross.

Why the line is sharp

Rule B is exactly site-bond percolation: fire crosses when a chain of trees joined by "successful" links spans the forest. On an infinite lattice the chance of that jumps from 0 to 1 at the critical line. At p = 1 it sits at D = 0.5927; at D = 1 it sits at p = 1/2.

Try this

Set p to 1 and slide D through 0.59: the burned area goes from a thin strip to the whole forest. Then press Test 200 fires a little below and a little above the line, and switch the forest size to see the transition sharpen.
